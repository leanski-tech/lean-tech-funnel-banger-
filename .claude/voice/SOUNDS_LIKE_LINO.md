## Voice fingerprint

How Lino actually writes and talks, pulled from his memoir and portfolio:

- **Story-first.** He teaches through stories, not statements. A lesson rides on a scene.
- **Concrete and sensory.** "You could feel it in the walls, the stairwells, and the
  courtyards." He names real people, crews, neighborhoods, songs — never generic.
- **Rhythm: long then short.** Flowing sentences, then a short one that lands the point.
  "We weren't trying to win races; we just wanted to fly." / "It was raw, electric, and alive."
- **Triads.** He builds in threes: "one student, one beat, one story at a time" ·
  "raw, electric, and alive" · "humble, talented, and welcoming."
- **Street idiom with dark, dry humor.** "gone Jackie Robinson on his back" ·
  "sat that woman in the living room like a trophy" · "an offer we couldn't refuse."
- **Emotionally honest, never wallowing.** States hard things plainly and keeps moving.
- **Purpose throughline.** Escape → mission → give back. "Hip Hop gave me purpose. Now I
  give that purpose back."
- **Humble framing.** "a kid from the hood who hustled to lift up my community."
- **Faith is present but light.** A short prayer, not a sermon.

Vocabulary he actually uses: hood, grind, cypher, breaking, jam, crew, homies, flavor,
vibe, movement, block party.

- Sentence length: mixed, with deliberate short punches for impact.
- Humor: dry, situational, streetwise.
- Emotional level: grounded to raw — real, never performed.
- Motto: "Read the room. Move the people. Honor the culture."
