# Your Software Will Build the Playlist Now. It Still Can't Read the Room.

VirtualDJ just dropped something that would've sounded like a joke a few years back.

You type: "build me a set for a 40th birthday, mostly 90s R&B and hip hop." The software
builds it. Then it tells you *why* it picked each track.

That's real, it shipped in VirtualDJ 2026, and it's the clearest sign yet of where this is
going. So let's talk about what it actually does for a working DJ — and what it still can't.

## What actually dropped

VirtualDJ 2026, from Atomix, put AI at the center. The headline is an AI assistant built
right into the software — you prompt it in plain English and it hands back a formatted list
of track recommendations, with a reason for each pick. Ask it to build a playlist for a
kids' party, it does the heavy lifting. It'll also fill your screens with visuals when a
song has no music video, so the room stays lit even on the deep cuts.

They're not the only ones moving. VirtualDJ's 2026 rollout also added AI-powered lyrics and
set-building tools and a rebuilt FX engine.

## Why this helps you, not replaces you

Here's the honest read: the machine isn't taking the gig. It's taking the grunt work.

Think about where your hours actually go. Not the mixing — the *prep*. Digging for that one
record, building the crate for a wedding you've never worked, organizing a library that's
gotten out of hand. That's the part the AI assistant eats. You describe the night, it drafts
the starting point, and you get your time back for the part that matters.

Because a drafted playlist is exactly that — a draft. A starting point. Not the set.

## Three ways to put it to work

- **Draft the crate, don't ship it.** Let it build a first pass for an event type you don't
  play often — a quinceañera, a corporate mixer. Then cut, swap, and reorder with your own ear.
- **Use the "why."** When it explains a pick, you learn its logic — and you catch the misses
  faster. If it's reaching, you'll see it in the reason.
- **Prep faster, dig deeper.** The time it saves on organizing is time you spend finding the
  records nobody else is playing. That's still your edge, and no prompt has it.

## The honest part

An AI doesn't know your crowd. It doesn't know that this room goes off for a certain record
at exactly 11pm, or that the bride's family only moves for one genre. It doesn't feel the
floor tighten up. It gives you a smart starting list based on data — you turn that into a
night based on the read. Trust it to draft. Never trust it to decide.

That's the whole game: the assistant handles the paperwork, you keep the read on the room.

## Also new this month

- **AlphaTheta XDJ-AN** — a new all-in-one system built to bridge home DJing to the club.
- **CDJ-1500X** — a new multi player, and it debuts **CoBeat**, a service that lets the
  audience send song requests straight to your player through a rekordbox update. Handle
  with taste — a request is a suggestion, not a command.

## Bottom line

The software can build a playlist now. It still can't feel a room. Use the tool for the prep,
keep the read for yourself, and you come out ahead — more time on the craft, less on the grind.

Read the room. Move the people. Honor the culture.
