# The Open-Format Party Spine — works any room

> By Jazzy Jeff (music curation) for DJ Leanskee. A reusable open-format framework, not a
> rigid tracklist — built to flex across weddings, corporate, and private parties. Records
> are real and nameable. **BPMs are approximate; confirm exact BPM + musical key in Mixed In
> Key / Tunebat before you lock a set.** Fresh-now picks verified via live web 2026-07-26.

## The idea
One set can't be a fixed list if it plays every room. So this is a **spine**: four phases by
energy, each with a BPM zone and a *menu* across generations and genres. You pull from the
menu to match the crowd in front of you. That's open format — the arc stays, the records move.

---

## Phase 1 — Warm-Up / Dinner (~90–110 BPM)
**Job:** set a groove, don't fill the floor yet. Heads nod, nobody feels pushed.
- Soul/funk bed: Earth Wind & Fire, Stevie Wonder, Al Green, D'Angelo.
- Grown & smooth: Anthony Hamilton, Sade, early-2000s R&B.
- Neutral crossover: Bruno Mars mid-tempo, H.E.R., Silk Sonic.
- *Move:* keep it in one BPM lane so conversation flows; you're building trust, not peaks.

## Phase 2 — Pull-In / Build (~108–120 BPM)
**Job:** first bodies up. Play the ones nobody can sit through.
- Guaranteed early movers: "Uptown Funk," "Can't Stop the Feeling," "I Wanna Dance With Somebody."
- Group-participation triggers (they already know the moves): Cupid Shuffle, Cha Cha Slide,
  Wobble — deploy one, not all; it's a spark, not a crutch.
- Generational bridge: a Motown/disco cut into a modern funk-pop record so every age claims it.
- *Move:* this is where you read who's in the room. Lean the menu toward the crowd that's up.

## Phase 3 — Peak (~118–128, with drops and swings)
**Job:** the party they'll talk about. Anthems + the open-format flex.
- Universal peak: "September," disco floor-fillers, big singalongs ("Mr. Brightside" if the
  room's got that rock DNA).
- Hip Hop / R&B peak block: throw it back — 90s/2000s the whole room shouts, then bridge to now.
- The flex: the unexpected blend that shouldn't work and does — a genre jump landed on matched
  key/BPM. That's the moment that makes you the DJ they book again.
- *Move:* ride the energy in waves — peak, small drop, higher peak. A flat wall of bangers tires
  a floor; contrast keeps it alive.

## Phase 4 — Land / Late (~100–115, or drop to close)
**Job:** bring it home so they leave full, not worn out.
- Late-night grown folks: soul, slow jams, a reggae one-drop to cool the room.
- The closer: one everybody sings with the lights coming up — send them out on a feeling.
- *Move:* don't chase one more peak past the room's energy. Land it clean.

---

## Harmonic mixing — the science under the feel
- **Key:** mix in-key using the Camelot wheel — same key, ±1 on the wheel, or the relative
  major/minor. Jump +7 (or the "energy boost") when you want lift. In-key blends sound
  intentional; clashing keys sound like an accident.
- **BPM:** blend inside a ~±5% range, or use half/double-time to bridge a hip-hop record (~90)
  into a house/dance record (~120–125) cleanly.
- **Always confirm** each record's real key + BPM in Mixed In Key or Tunebat before the gig.
  Never guess a key live — that's the one thing that turns a flex into a train wreck.

## Adapt the spine per room
- **Wedding:** more singalongs and generational bridges; keep it clean; land on romance.
- **Corporate:** cleaner edits, safer peaks, tighter time blocks; read the open-bar curve.
- **Private / cultural (quinceañera, family):** more Latin, Afrobeat, and reggae in the menu;
  program to the family's roots, not a template.

*Read the room. Move the people. Honor the culture.*
